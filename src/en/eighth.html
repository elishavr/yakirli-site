The Day After Shiva

The Day After Shiva aims to build contact and support for families after shiva and in the years that follow. Support is coordinated after the family contacts Yakir Li or the local authority, taking into account their wishes, needs and the support available.

A caring community for families facing civilian bereavement

The Day After Shiva gives families who have experienced a civilian loss one-to-one and group support, help claiming their rights and benefits, and emotional support over time. When needed, families can also be referred for financial and legal advice.

Professionally trained volunteers stay in touch with the families, in local authorities and communities across Israel.

The day after shiva

What happens to the family when shiva ends

The day after shiva, a heavy silence fills the home. The people around them return to routine, and the family is left with the loss – emotionally and in everyday functioning.

The emotional layer

  • Loneliness, exhaustion, confusion and shock, anxiety and despair
  • Outbursts of suppressed emotions
  • Family difficulties
  • Trauma – post-traumatic stress

The functional layer

  • Difficulty with daily functioning, depression
  • Decline at work, up to unemployment
  • Decline in studies, up to dropping out

Our goals

Emotional and practical support

Staying alongside the family as those around them return to everyday life.

Resilience and a sense of support

Strengthening family members' sense of support and emotional resilience.

Rights and benefits

Helping families claim their rights and easing the bureaucratic burden.

How can families get help and support?

Home visits

Personal meetings in the family's home, at a pace that suits them.

Phone calls

Staying in touch, even when meeting is too much.

Informal meetings

A conversation, coffee or an outing, depending on what suits the family.

Community commitment

Local volunteers who connect the family to its community.

Flow chart

Bringing the program to a local authority – step by step

How the program works: from the local authority to the volunteers, and from the volunteers to the families.

  1. 1

    Approaching the local authority

    • Initial contact with the volunteer, social services or community department
    • Presenting the program and checking feasibility
  2. 2

    Approval and coordinating support

    • Briefing meeting with the relevant officials
    • Securing organizational and logistical backing
    • Budgeting the activity (mainly volunteer training)
  3. 3

    Recruiting volunteers

    • Appointing a local volunteer coordinator
    • Recruiting volunteers from the community
    • Volunteer interviews
  4. 4

    Training volunteers

    • 3–5 in-person sessions led by a social worker
    • Active listening, empathy, support tools, boundaries and referral to professionals
    • Ongoing support and retention of volunteers
  5. 5

    Reaching families who have experienced loss

    • General publicity in the community (notices, flyers, social media)
    • Referrals through the volunteers themselves
  6. 6

    Support and accompaniment

    • Contacting the family for emotional support, a listening ear and practical help
    • Close support from the local authority and Yakir Li, with professional help as needed
  7. 7

    Continuity

    • Retaining volunteers through team-building and enrichment meetings
    • Workshops and training throughout the year
    • A supportive community for the volunteers themselves
    • Involving the family and learning from the field

The key message: a community that stays in touch with families who have experienced loss, after shiva and beyond.
