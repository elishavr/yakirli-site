Resilience circles in education

Support circles and tools for coping with loss – for school staff supporting students who have lost a family member.

About the program

The GEFEN program (support circles and tools for coping with loss) is designed for teachers supporting students who have lost a family member. It builds circles of resilience around the grieving student: training for school staff, support from classmates, meetings with the school psychologist, and family workshops.

The goal is to help the student return to school and social life with full support, while creating a safe space to express feelings. The program accompanies the school throughout the year: preparing the class, responding to difficult memorial dates, and strengthening belonging and community.

Program goals

Emotional support

Helping grieving students return to school and social life.

A stronger community

Encouraging staff and students to support their friends with respect and sensitivity.

A safe space

Making room for grief and solidarity, adapted to age and personal needs.

Program components

1. Preparing the school staff

  • Training and guidance: teaching staff about the stages of grief and giving them tools to support the student.
  • A personal plan: support tailored to the student, including academic and social adjustments.

2. An initial conversation with the family

  • A personal meeting: agreeing with the student and parents on the support needed.
  • Information for staff: sharing the appropriate information to ensure a supportive environment.

3. Emotional preparation of the class

  • An introductory lesson: the homeroom teacher prepares the class to support the student sensitively.
  • Practical ways to help: giving classmates tools to support a grieving friend.

4. Ongoing support through the year

  • A personal support group: meetings with close friends to share feelings.
  • Professional meetings: sessions with the school psychologist for follow-up and support.
  • Class activities: social activities that strengthen belonging.

5. Open conversation all year

  • Homeroom lessons: discussions about sensitivity and loss, adapted to the student’s needs.
  • Memorial dates: preparing for difficult dates with group support.

6. Support beyond school

  • Family workshops: activities that help with the emotional work of grief.
  • Professional partnerships: access to counselling and resources from expert organizations.

A support workshop for school staff

Support circles and tools for coping with loss in formal and informal education – practical knowledge for responding professionally and sensitively, with a focus on students’ wellbeing and a supportive environment.

Understanding loss and its impact

Types of bereavement and their effect on the individual and the family; how children understand death at different ages.

Recognizing distress and responding

Tools for recognizing signs of distress in grieving children and knowing when to refer to professionals; emotional regulation; creating a supportive classroom.

Approaches to support

Key theories of supporting grieving children; open conversation, remembrance and rituals in the classroom; staff self-care and guidance for parents.

Practical workshop and summary

Discussion and shared experience, understanding the unique needs of grieving children, and building a support plan that includes parents and the community.
