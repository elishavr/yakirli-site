Legislation and rights

Yakir Li works in the Knesset, with government ministries and with public bodies for full recognition of families who have suffered loss and bereavement.

Our achievements

  • A death grant for families bereaved of a child (up to age 18) who received a disability allowance
  • Vocational rehabilitation for parents who lost their job during the illness of their child who died (up to age 18) and received a disability allowance
  • The "Ambulance Law" – exemption from ambulance fees for a patient who died in the ambulance or the emergency room
  • Extended retirement age for bereaved parents – the option to stay at work 4 more years beyond retirement age
  • Eased service conditions (TASH 3) for soldiers whose family member died in circumstances of civilian bereavement
  • Soldiers and national service volunteers who died during their service – their heirs receive the discharge grant

In the legislative process

  • A paid day off on the family's private memorial day

How many people have benefited?

Unfortunately, we do not have verified figures. Data on actual take-up is held by the National Insurance Institute and the bodies responsible for implementation, and is not collected by us, so we do not publish a number of recipients.

What we can show is the potential. According to National Insurance Institute data, about 250 children who received a disabled child allowance die each year. This means that since 2016 about 2,500 families have become eligible for the death grant, and about 2,200 of them can also apply for vocational rehabilitation.

These are estimates of eligible families, not a count of those who actually claimed the right. In addition, we have documented dozens of personal inquiries from families. This number reflects only documented inquiries and does not capture the full impact of our work.
